# The Five Constitutional Substrates

## Core separation

LocalBrain separates **institutional truth** from **machine reasoning**.

Five deterministic substrates preserve what an institution is. Executive Intelligence may reason over those records. It may not replace them.

```text
Episode          records history.
Artifact         preserves evidence.
Fact             accepts institutional knowledge.
Conversation     preserves interpretation.
DecisionCitation records exercised authority.
```

None of them execute anything.

---

## The five substrates

| Substrate | Preserves | Integrity | Executive question |
| --------- | --------- | --------- | ------------------ |
| **Episode** | History | Historical fidelity | What happened? |
| **Artifact** | Evidence | Authenticity (A13) | What evidence exists? |
| **Fact** | Institutional knowledge | Explainability (A12) | What do we know? |
| **Conversation** | Interpretation | Context · Sequence · Attribution (A14–A16) | What were people saying? |
| **DecisionCitation** | Exercised authority | Authority (A17) | Why did we act? |

Each substrate owns exactly one kind of institutional truth.

---

## Substrate progression

```text
Reality
      ↓
Episode          Something happened.
      ↓
Artifact         Evidence exists.
      ↓
Fact             The institution accepts this as knowledge.
      ↓
Conversation     People interpreted the knowledge.
      ↓
DecisionCitation The institution exercised authority.
```

Knowledge does not automatically create authority. Authority does not automatically create truth. Interpretation does not automatically become knowledge.

## Constitutional principles

### Memory records. Intelligence interprets. Policy acts.

| Layer | Responsibility | Must not |
| ----- | -------------- | -------- |
| **Memory** | Preserve institutional records | Infer, decide, or act |
| **Intelligence** | Reason over records | Store authoritative truth |
| **Policy** | Authorize action | Bypass audit or consent |

### Authority (DecisionCitation)

> **Authority is exercised. It is never inferred.**

> **Authority is recorded. It is never reconstructed.**

> **Authority creates responsibility. It does not create truth.**

Decision Ledger owns binding authority. Memory stores DecisionCitation only.

### Interpretation (Conversation)

> **Interpretation survives disagreement.**

Conversation preserves what people said. It does not reconcile disagreement into knowledge or authority.

### Knowledge (Fact)

> **Knowledge must never become detached from evidence.**

Facts represent institutional acceptance — not objective truth independent of provenance.

### Evidence (Artifact)

> **Evidence only — never conclusions.**

Artifacts preserve what existed. Authenticity survives custody.

---

## Reference-not-ownership

Every substrate references outward. No substrate owns another.

```text
DecisionCitation  →  may reference  →  Episode · Artifact · Fact · Conversation
DecisionCitation  →  never owns     →  Episode · Artifact · Fact · Conversation
```

The same rule applies across all five substrates.

---

## Deterministic foundation

Given the same inputs, canonical storage always produces the same outputs at the substrate layer.

```text
Institutional Cognition Foundation  →  Deterministic
Executive Intelligence              →  Advisory
Policy                              →  Authoritative (action)
```

When the model changes, the substrates do not change. Only the reasoning layer changes. That is intentional.

---

## Defining sentence

> **AI should not be the memory of an institution. AI should reason over the institution's deterministic memory.**

---

## Philosophical conclusion

> Institutions should not depend on the memory of machines.
>
> Institutions should preserve their own history, evidence, knowledge, interpretation, and authority in deterministic form.
>
> Intelligence should reason over those records—not replace them.
>
> Models will evolve. Institutions should endure.
