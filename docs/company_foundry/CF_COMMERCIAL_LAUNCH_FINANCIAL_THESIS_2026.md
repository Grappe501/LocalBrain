# Company Foundry — November 2026 Commercial Launch & Financial Thesis

Status: Working planning assumptions. Not a promise, valuation, securities offering, or accounting opinion.

## Launch posture
Launch Company Foundry as three reinforcing businesses: product company, paid implementation/build studio, and Academy/builder foundry.

Do not launch the entire portfolio simultaneously. Recommended November posture: one flagship commercial product, one paid institutional implementation/pilot offer, and one controlled Cohort 1.

## Initial capital
Working capitalization: $20,000.

Illustrative allocation:
- $8,000 GPU server / infrastructure
- $8,000 intern/training payroll = 400 hours at $20/hour
- $2,000 legal/accounting/contracts/compliance reserve
- $2,000 operating contingency

Recommended Cohort 1: 3–5 builders. Example: four builders × 10 hours/week × 10 weeks = 400 paid hours.

## Cash-flow thesis
Near-term cash should be driven primarily by founder-led institutional pilots, implementations, customization, and high-value bounded builds rather than waiting for mass-market subscription scale.

Targets:
- first invoice: within 30 days
- meaningful cash receipts: 30–60 days
- $5K MRR/run-rate: 60–120 days
- operating cash-flow break-even target: months 4–7
- exit MRR target after first 12 months: $15K–$25K; working target $20K

## January 31, 2027 validation checkpoint
Evidence that would justify materially raising expectations:
1. 2–3 paying institutional customers;
2. one recurring product showing real usage/revenue traction;
3. functioning Cohort 1;
4. evidence trainees can complete independently accepted production phases.

If those exist, a $250K–$500K first-year outcome becomes more credible. If the company instead has substantial software but no paying external customer, reduce discretionary burn and prioritize sales/customer discovery over additional speculative product construction.

## Flywheel
Existing IP → commercial prioritization → pilots/subscriptions → cash → controlled trainee hours → increased development capacity → graduates → Capstone candidates → selected Foundry-financed pilots → FAB recovery → post-recovery residual economics → Company portfolio participation → finance additional products/cohorts.

## Commercial discipline
The critical launch risk is lack of focus, not lack of software. The portfolio should be treated as option value. Commercial proof determines which options receive additional capital.

The first economic milestone is proving that one dollar entering Company Foundry can reproduce itself while increasing productive capacity and owned/participating intellectual property.

## Financial/legal boundary
All figures are planning hypotheses. Before live equity, residual settlement, payroll structures, Foundry advances, revenue-sharing, or unusual compensation instruments are activated, obtain appropriate legal, tax, securities, employment, and accounting review.
