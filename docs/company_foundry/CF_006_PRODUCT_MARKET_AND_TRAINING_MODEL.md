**Status:** BASELINE COMPLETE — market assumptions require continuing validation

## Doctrine expansion

Every commercially exploitable output is a **Product Project**. This includes software, books, research publications, education products, data products, templates, and other IP-bearing outputs admitted by Company Foundry.

A book may therefore be admitted under a Master Build Plan, assigned a team, built through phases (research, canon, outline, drafting, editing, fact-checking, design, publishing, audio, launch, marketing), and—when it is an approved Capstone—use the same residual architecture as software. Co-authors and production contributors may receive defined residual percentages without automatically receiving ownership of Company Foundry.

Copyright authorship, work-made-for-hire, joint authorship, moral rights where applicable, publishing rights, ISBN/imprint ownership, audiobook rights, adaptation rights, foreign rights, and contributor agreements must be explicit before a book team begins material production.

## Valuation method used in this baseline

This file does **not** claim company valuations. It estimates conservative **annual revenue capacity** for products after reaching a credible market foothold.

For every project, management first forms a deliberately conservative attainable-revenue case and then applies an additional **50% Foundry haircut**. The number below is therefore the *haircut case*, not the optimistic case and not TAM.

These figures are planning assumptions for capital allocation. They must be replaced by customer/sales data as products launch.

## Current market anchors

- Consumer meal-planning competitors generally charge single-digit monthly subscriptions; Samsung Food+ currently lists $6.99/month or $59.99/year and advertises 180,000+ recipes.
- Political/organizing SaaS supports materially higher pricing. NationBuilder lists Starter at $34/month and Pro at $160/month; Ecanvasser lists tiers from roughly $99/month into high-hundreds/thousands depending on database/territory.
- Professional AI knowledge/workspace tools span low-cost personal subscriptions to premium proactive/enterprise offerings; Mem lists Pro at $12/month and Proactive at $99/month.
- Author software commonly spans roughly $10–$50/month, while non-AI writing tools may use one-time licensing.
- Amazon KDP states eligible ebooks can earn up to 70% royalties and print books up to 60% before applicable delivery/printing costs.

### Book economics

Book residuals use the same principle as software, but the waterfall is publishing-specific:

`publisher/platform receipts`
`- refunds/returns`
`- printing/delivery/distribution`
`- direct advertising/launch spend`
`- editing/design/narration obligations`
`- approved Foundry shared-services allocation`
`- approved reserves/recoupment`
`= Book Distributable Residual`

For an accepted book Capstone, Company Foundry retains at least 25% of Book Distributable Residual, the Capstone lead receives no more than 51%, and co-authors/editors/research/production team members can receive defined residual percentages. Copyright and publishing ownership remain governed by the signed Book Product Agreement rather than inferred from the residual percentage.

A co-author may also be the Capstone lead, team member, or outside rights holder. Those roles must not be conflated.

## First recommended training project — SousChef Beta Commercialization

### Why SousChef

SousChef remains the best first training project because:

- it is already approximately 90% technically ready;
- remaining work is commercially important but bounded;
- it is outside electoral politics, reducing compliance complexity for the first cohort;
- trainees can touch billing, onboarding, analytics, privacy, UX, AI-cost controls, testing, deployment, and customer feedback;
- failure of an individual phase is recoverable without risking an election operation;
- competitors provide clear price anchors.

### Conservative pricing hypothesis

Market anchor: Samsung Food+ currently lists **$6.99/month or $59.99/year**.

Foundry launch hypothesis:

- Free/basic acquisition tier
- Founding Household: **$5.99/month**
- Annual: **$49/year**
- Later premium household/AI tier only after usage economics are measured

This deliberately prices below the major reference point while we learn retention and AI cost.

## Turning the remaining 10% into an actual training budget

A readiness percentage is not automatically a dollar percentage, so CF-006 creates a **Launch Completion Budget** rather than pretending 10% of code equals 10% of historical cost.

### Cohort-1 SousChef launch budget: **$12,000 direct build budget**

Working labor model:

- 3 apprentices × 120 production/training hours × $20/hr = **$7,200**
- 1 qualified lead/reviewer × 60 hours × $40/hr phase-equivalent = **$2,400**
- beta tooling/testing/AI/API/app-store/design allowance = **$1,200**
- contingency/rework reserve = **$1,200**

**Total = $12,000**

This is the budget to convert the estimated remaining ~10% into a commercially testable V1, not a valuation of the 90% already built.

## SousChef revenue model — actual conservative numbers

Use a blended realized subscription revenue assumption of **$4.50 per paying household per month** after annual-plan discounting/promotions but before payment/AI/support costs.

| Paying households | Monthly realized revenue | Annual realized revenue |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | $450 | $5,400 |
| 250 | $1,125 | $13,500 |
| 500 | $2,250 | $27,000 |
| 1,000 | $4,500 | $54,000 |
| 2,500 | $11,250 | $135,000 |
| 5,000 | $22,500 | $270,000 |
| 10,000 | $45,000 | $540,000 |

The **haircut capacity band of $150k–$600k/year** corresponds roughly to 2,800–11,100 paying-household equivalents at the $4.50 realized monthly assumption. This is an attainable-capacity scenario, not a forecast that those customers will appear.

### Break-even against the $12,000 launch-completion budget

Ignoring ongoing operating costs for the moment:

- 500 paid households at $4.50 realized/month = $2,250/month → 5.3 months of revenue equals $12,000.
- 1,000 paid households = $4,500/month → 2.7 months.
- 2,500 paid households = $11,250/month → about 1.1 months.

The real break-even will be later because AI, hosting, support, payment fees, marketing, taxes, and continuing labor must be deducted. The beta must measure these rather than assume them.

## Example residual economics after V1

Illustrative mature month only:

- 5,000 paying households × $4.50 realized = **$22,500 revenue**
- payment/refund allowance (5%) = **-$1,125**
- AI/data/hosting (12%) = **-$2,700**
- customer support/ops (8%) = **-$1,800**
- marketing/growth allocation (15%) = **-$3,375**
- Foundry shared services (10%) = **-$2,250**
- operating reserve (10%) = **-$2,250**
- **Illustrative DPR = $9,000/month**

If this were an accepted Capstone using a 40% lead / 25% team / 35% company allocation:

- Capstone lead = **$3,600/month**
- team pool = **$2,250/month**
- Company Foundry = **$3,150/month**

SousChef itself predates the Academy and is therefore **not automatically someone's Capstone**. This calculation demonstrates the engine only. Cohort 1 works on SousChef as a training/production project unless Foundry governance explicitly admits a new qualifying Capstone.

## Key conclusion

The portfolio should no longer be modeled as “software projects plus some books.” It is an **IP portfolio**. Software, books, research products, courses, datasets, and future media can all use the same Master Plan → phases → team → acceptance → revenue → residual architecture, while each product class gets its own rights and cost waterfall.
